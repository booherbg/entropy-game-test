a garden you coax into matching

a flowering patch and a colony of pollinators are two living machines, each the other’s reproductive organ. up close, every flower is a colour-grid maze; a forager reads it with its inherited key. read it well → rich nectar, strong pollination.

nobody paints the patterns. you only set conditions — and natural selection drifts the flowers’ grids and the foragers’ keys toward each other until they merge into an exquisitely-fit pair. that climb, from fumbling to native, is the whole of it.

the world breathes on its own. pause to make weighed moves. it’s lovely to let it run and come back.
