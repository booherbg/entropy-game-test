mechanism 1 · generative presence

every flower is grown from a genome — pixel art, endless variety

no two alike. hue, petal count, core, and the nectar-guide pattern are all heritable. colour is energy — pixels are the prime resource, so the world is literally made of them.

mechanism 1·b · the keystone — genotype → phenotype

how a genome becomes a flower (and its decode-grid)

~8 genes: FORM (symmetry, petal length, sharpness, core) + SIGNAL (palette colours + nectar-guide pattern). expression is a polar function — every pixel's distance + angle decides core / petal / guide / empty; petals repeat symmetry times (that radial repeat is why a flower looks like a flower). the SAME genome, sampled down one petal-wedge, IS the decode-grid the pollinator reads. flower = its bloom, grid = its fingerprint — one genome, two renderings. drag a gene and watch both change together.

the unroll · how the round bloom becomes the flat decode-grid

because the flower is radially symmetric, all its signal lives in one petal-wedge. take that pie-slice and flatten it — radius → rows, angle → columns — then downsample to a 6×6 of palette colours. that's the fingerprint the pollinator reads.

mutate one gene → the bloom, the unroll, and the fingerprint all drift together
mechanism 2 · the signal lock-and-key — THE soul

the flower's maze and the pollinator's key co-evolve until they merge

up close, a flower is a colourful grid the pollinator must decode to reach the nectar. reward ≈ how well its key matches the maze. selection drifts them toward each other — watch the two grids converge and the fit climb from fumbling to native.

lock-and-key fit: — · —

selection + mutation only — nobody is painting the grids. lock the flower and the key chases it; unlock and they meet in the middle. split a population and they'd diverge into two species.

mechanism 3 · the colony, made emergent

beacons draw foragers; trails make a mind

from afar, the beacon (the flower's colour) draws pollinators whose preference matches. a forager that finds nectar hauls it home and lays a recruitment trail; others follow the strongest trails. no leader — the colony's behaviour emerges.

dots tint toward the flower they prefer (camouflage you can read); faint paths are trails; the ring is the colony, brightening as nectar banks.

mechanism 4 · one currency

energy, embodied as light and colour

a richer flower costs the tree more sugar — every trait is a real trade-off, so selection has teeth. maintenance leaks as heat (the second law); the world is bounded by light in ÷ losses.

mechanism 5 · progress = the tree grows niches

the unlock tree is literally a tree

spend the tree's sugar to grow a new niche — a new flower, a sap-well, shelter. each is a new lock awaiting a key → a new specialist to discover. depth breeds width on its own.
